What is different

Running the model is the easy part.

A model small enough for a phone can reason well and still fail at real work. It picks the wrong tool, drowns in what a service sends back, or stops halfway. Most apps leave that to the model. Resident treats it as the app's job.

A typical local chat app Resident
Tools None, or a handful built in Web search, page reading, code, memory, skills, your calendar and reminders
Your services Not connected GitHub, Linear, Notion and anything else that speaks MCP, with a proper sign-in

With the app closed Nothing happens Runs from Shortcuts, on a schedule if you like
How you know it works You try it and see 388 scored test requests, and the code, are public

It finishes what it starts

Resident runs a tool, reads what came back and decides what to do next, for as many steps as the job takes. The chat is saved after every step, so a job cut off by a phone call carries on from where it was, without doing anything twice.

It signs in to your real accounts

Add a service and you sign in on that service's own page, the way you would for any app. Resident never sees a password, keeps the access it is given in the iPhone's Keychain, and renews it without asking you again.

Tool calls come out right

Once a model starts to call a tool, Resident lets it write only a tool that exists with arguments that fit. If a call still fails, the model is told why and what the tool expects, so it can correct itself instead of giving up.

Everything is cut to fit

A small model has little room to think in. Resident shows it a service's tools only when a request needs them, shortens long answers while keeping every item, and summarises old parts of a chat, so the tenth question is as quick as the first.

The chores are done for it

A small model busy answering will not stop to remember a fact about you or name the chat. Resident does each as a short request of its own after the reply, so they happen every time.

You see every step

Each search, page and tool call is shown as it happens, with what was sent and what came back. When an answer is wrong you can see where it went wrong.

What it does

An agent, not a chat box.

Looks things up

Searches the web and reads pages when a question needs something recent.

Works it out

Runs code in a sandbox for arithmetic, dates and data, so answers are computed, not guessed.

Uses your apps

Calendar and Reminders on the device. Linear, Notion and anything else that speaks MCP, by pasting an address.

Remembers you

Keeps a short list of lasting facts you mention. Read it, edit it or switch it off.

Learns your way

Turn something it just did into a skill with one tap. You see and approve every skill before it is kept.

Reads what you give it

Photos, PDFs and documents, and dictation by a speech model that also runs on the device.

Agents

Opinionated, and yours to change.

An agent is a set of instructions, the tools it may use and how hard it thinks. Start with the Assistant, switch to the Coder, or make your own for the things you do often.

The defaults are meant to be right. The numbers are there when you want them: temperature and reply length per agent, sampling per model, thinking per chat.

Automation

Runs from Shortcuts, in the background.

The Ask Resident action takes a message, an agent and a model, and hands back the reply as plain text. A model on your phone answers without the app ever opening. Chain it with anything, or run it on a schedule.

Jobs that take a while show their progress as a Live Activity, with a button to stop them.
